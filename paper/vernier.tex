\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Commercial dataset vendors increasingly certify corpus quality with single-pass vision-language
model statistics. Build AI reports that its Egocentric-10K corpus shows $\geq$1 hand in 96.42\%
of frames and active manipulation in 91.66\%, leading Ego4D \citep{grauman2022ego4d} and
EPIC-KITCHENS-100 \citep{damen2022epic100}, from 10{,}000 frames labelled once by
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} with no human gold, no confidence interval, and no cross-domain check.
Against \vernierNPrimary{} single-rater gold frames scored under a written rubric, the vendor's
stored labels over-call $\geq$1 hand on 9 of 24 at-risk frames (37.5\%, 95\% Wilson
$[21.2, 57.3]$) and under-call 0 of 129 ($[0.0, 2.9]$, $p = 1.3\times 10^{-8}$), and over-call
active manipulation on 10 of 33 (30.3\%) against 4 of 120 (3.3\%, $p = 3.4\times 10^{-5}$). An
independent open judge (\texttt{Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-FP8}) shows the same asymmetry (6/24 vs.\
0/129; 10/33 vs.\ 2/120). Second, on two 10{,}000-frame raw-corpus draws, camera-wearer
clustering yields design effects of 1.25 to 1.66, widening intervals by 12 to 29\% over iid
(post-hoc factory clustering reaches 2.18 to 5.65); because evaluation releases omit grouping
IDs, direct cluster-robust inference cannot run on the published sample. Third, PPI++
gold-correction over the vendor's 9{,}800-frame stored arms moves the active-manipulation lead
over EPIC-KITCHENS-100 from $+6.62$ to $-1.32$\,pp (iid 95\% CI $[-10.78, +8.14]$; post-hoc
cluster sensitivity $[-11.28, +8.64]$ to $[-12.07, +9.43]$), leaving the lead unresolved.
Resolving it takes roughly 89 gold frames per arm under iid (55 more than exist) and 93 to 115
under clustering. Because all \vernierNPrimary{} labels are single-rater and the vendor's judge
reverses on EPIC-KITCHENS-100 manipulation, an independent second rater is needed before
generalising this asymmetry into an upper-bound claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Commercial video dataset vendors publish quality metrics alongside their corpora to justify
training value and differentiate against academic benchmarks. Build AI's evaluation release for
Egocentric-10K reports that 96.42\% of frames contain at least one hand, 76.34\% contain two
hands, and 91.66\% show active manipulation, placing the corpus ahead of both Ego4D
\citep{grauman2022ego4d} and EPIC-KITCHENS-100 \citep{damen2022epic100}. The underlying corpus is
substantial and unusually transparent by commercial standards: roughly 10{,}000 hours of factory
video from 2{,}153 workers across 85 factories, per-clip metadata, camera intrinsics, and an
ungated evaluation release shipping the judged frames and per-frame labels.

The advertised quality numbers themselves are bare point estimates produced by a single pass of
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} over 10{,}000 frames per corpus. They carry no human validation, no
uncertainty interval, and no check that the judge maintains equal accuracy on factory floors and
domestic kitchens. The comparative claim against EPIC-KITCHENS-100 rests on margins of 6.05
percentage points on $\geq$1 hand and 6.62 percentage points on active manipulation---small enough
that asymmetric or domain-varying judge error could account for most of the gap.

We conducted a pre-registered measurement audit of this quality claim using Build AI's released
evaluation frames, its stored per-frame \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} labels, a self-hosted
open-weights judge (\texttt{Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-FP8}), \vernierNPrimary{} human gold labels from
one rater applying a 22-rule written rubric, and two 10{,}000-frame draws from the raw video
corpus carrying real worker and factory identifiers. Our contributions are fourfold:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Strongly asymmetric judge over-calling in both the vendor's stored labels and an
      open judge.} On the audited $\geq$1-hand indicator, the vendor's own stored labels over-call
      9 of 24 at-risk frames and under-call 0 of 129 ($p = 1.3\times 10^{-8}$); on active
      manipulation, 10 of 33 against 4 of 120 ($p = 3.4\times 10^{-5}$). An independent
      open-weights model shows the same pattern (6/24 vs.\ 0/129; 10/33 vs.\ 2/120). Separating
      the binary indicator from the ternary hand count isolates the one-against-two confusions
      that drive the two-hands discrepancy, and a rubric edge-case breakdown shows zero
      manipulation false positives on idle grip (\S\ref{sec:errdir}).
\item \textbf{Gold-corrected prevalences, unresolved comparative margins, and their labelling
      cost.} Applying prediction-powered inference (PPI++) \citep{angelopoulos2023ppiplusplus}
      over the vendor's full 9{,}800-frame unlabelled arms shifts the Egocentric-10K
      active-manipulation lead over EPIC-KITCHENS-100 from $+6.62$ to $-1.32$ percentage points
      (iid 95\% CI $[-10.78, +8.14]$), covering both zero and the published margin. Resolving
      whether the published margin is excluded requires roughly 89 gold frames per arm under iid
      (about 55 more than exist) and 93 to 115 under post-hoc cluster sensitivity
      (\S\ref{sec:prevalence}).
\item \textbf{Measured worker and factory clustering in an egocentric corpus, with cluster-robust
      sensitivity bounds.} Across two 10{,}000-frame raw-corpus draws, worker clustering yields
      design effects of 1.25 to 1.66 (widening intervals by 12 to 29\%, exact unequal-cluster
      ANOVA $\hat{\rho}_w = 0.039$ to $0.106$), and post-hoc factory clustering reaches 2.18 to
      5.65 (widening intervals by 48 to 138\%). Because evaluation frames omit grouping
      identifiers, direct Cluster-Robust PPI++ \citep{broska2026crppi} cannot be computed on the
      published sample; post-hoc sensitivity analysis shows how EPIC-KITCHENS-100's 37-participant
      cap induces an irreducible unlabelled variance floor (\S\ref{sec:cluster}).
\item \textbf{A repeatable, open evaluation pipeline and complete public record.} Running the
      self-hosted open judge costs roughly \$9 per 10{,}000 frames with no proprietary API; across
      both the 10K and 100K releases it lands within 1 percentage point on $\geq$1 hand and active
      manipulation while missing 2 hands by over 6 points (\S\ref{sec:secondary}). Every
      pre-registered hypothesis outcome and methodological deviation is recorded in
      Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Gold-corrected estimation and clustered inference with model labels.}
Prediction-powered inference (PPI) \citep{angelopoulos2023ppi} and PPI++
\citep{angelopoulos2023ppiplusplus} combine a small human-labelled calibration set with a large
model-labelled pool to construct valid confidence intervals without assuming an unbiased model.
Confidence-driven inference \citep{gligoric2025cdi} concentrates human annotation where model
uncertainty is highest. Recent work adapts these tools to LLM evaluation:
\citet{divekar2026ranking} extends PPI to ranking metrics such as Precision@$K$, and
\citet{feng2026noisybutvalid} estimates judge true- and false-positive rates from a calibration
set to certify failure-rate bounds. Most closely related methodologically,
\citet{broska2026crppi} introduce Cluster-Robust PPI++, deriving closed-form standard errors and
cluster-aware power tuning for clustered and partially labelled data where standard iid PPI++
suffers severe undercoverage.

Our setting differs from prior model-evaluation studies in estimating a \emph{third-party
commercial corpus statistic} directly on that vendor's released frames and stored labels. In our
data, one judge error rate sits at or near zero---the special case of
\citet{feng2026noisybutvalid} in which naive judge estimates shift predominantly in one
direction---while the absence of cluster identifiers in the vendor's evaluation release blocks
direct application of \citet{broska2026crppi}, motivating our raw-corpus design-effect
measurement and post-hoc sensitivity analysis.

\paragraph{Dataset auditing and commercial claims.}
Re-annotating benchmark data to test published claims has a strong precedent:
\citet{beyer2020imagenet} re-label ImageNet validation images and find smaller real gains,
\citet{northcutt2021labelerrors} quantify label errors across ten benchmarks and show ranking
instability, and \citet{raji2019actionable} audit commercial vendors' accuracy claims directly.
Whereas \citet{beyer2020imagenet} and \citet{northcutt2021labelerrors} target label accuracy
against a fixed taxonomy, we audit vendor-authored summary statistics published without error bars
as a comparative selling point.

\paragraph{Judge agreement, prompt sensitivity, clustering, and egocentric benchmarks.}
\citet{rao2026agreement} show that agreement-reporting choices move judge--human metrics
substantially; we follow their reporting checklist and report Gwet's AC1 \citep{gwet2008ac1}
alongside Cohen's $\kappa$ because $\kappa$ becomes unstable above 90\% prevalence.
\citet{liu2026ipr} formalises inter-prompt reliability across prompt wordings, which our prompt
sweep adopts. \citet{miller2024errorbars} emphasises clustered standard errors in model
evaluation, building on classical survey design effects \citep{kish1965survey}; we apply this
to frame-level dataset statistics clustered by camera wearer and factory. For selective
distillation, \citet{jung2024trustorescalate} target human-agreement guarantees via Learn-then-Test
\citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}. Among egocentric benchmarks, HD-EPIC \citep{perrett2025hdepic}
operates a rigorous multi-annotator pipeline for temporal narrations, while EPIC-KITCHENS-100
\citep{damen2022epic100} and Ego4D \citep{grauman2022ego4d} do not attach confidence intervals or
judge-validation metrics to headline quality summaries. Across the egocentric dataset papers we
read in full, none reports a confidence interval on a headline corpus-quality statistic.

\section{Data, release structure, and protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Audited releases and metadata structure.}
Build AI's \texttt{Egocentric-10K-Evaluation} release ships three parquet files of 10{,}000 frames
each---Egocentric-10K, Ego4D, and EPIC-KITCHENS-100---containing image bytes alongside the
vendor's stored \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} per-frame \texttt{hand\_count} and
\texttt{active\_labor} labels. Shipping both frames and stored labels allows human gold to audit
the exact observations behind the published headline table without sampling mismatch. The
subsequent \texttt{Egocentric-100K-Evaluation} release also evaluates 10{,}000 frames for
Egocentric-100K while reusing identical Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS-100 baseline numbers.

Three structural facts bound external verification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{No cluster linkage in evaluation frames.} Every \texttt{frame\_id} in the 10K
      evaluation parquets is a bare UUID4 with no factory, worker, clip, or timestamp metadata.
      Neither an external auditor nor a buyer can verify the vendor's sampling frame or compute
      direct cluster-robust intervals on the evaluation parquets.
\item \emph{Removed identifier in the 100K release.} The \texttt{Egocentric-100K-Evaluation}
      parquet drops the \texttt{frame\_id} column altogether; we record this strictly as a
      reproducibility fact without inferring intent.
\item \emph{Raw-corpus worker reconciliation.} Indexing all 19{,}495 WebDataset shards of raw
      Egocentric-10K (192{,}903 clips, $\approx 16$\,TB) yields 2{,}144 distinct workers across 85
      factories against the card's 2{,}153 workers ($-0.42\%$), consistent with minor release-time
      filtering while factories, hours, and clip counts reconcile closely.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Frozen protocol, judges, and human rubric.}
All hypotheses, sample definitions, prompt variants, and stopping rules were frozen in
\texttt{docs/PRE-REGISTRATION.md} before any frame was fetched (seed 777 throughout). Every
subsequent deviation is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} and the public decision log.
Because \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} is deprecated for new API keys (confirmed via live HTTP 404),
live calls use a self-hosted open-weights model, \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-FP8}, with
temperature 0 and seed 777 pinned on every request and the vendor's prompts used verbatim.
Comparing against Build AI's published numbers therefore uses two arms: the vendor's own stored
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} labels and live \texttt{Qwen3-VL} outputs on the identical frames.

Ground-truth labels were collected by one rater (the author) on \vernierNPrimary{} frames drawn
from the 200-frame gold pools (63 Egocentric-10K, 60 EPIC-KITCHENS-100, 30 Ego4D) using a written
rubric of 22 numbered rules resolving ambiguities left open by the vendor's prompt (gloves,
reflections, motion blur, edge cropping, self-contact, third-party hands, and idle grip). The
labelling interface has no code path to judge outputs, enforcing judge blinding in software. A
34-pair blind intra-rater retest cleared the pre-registered 0.70 AC1 decidability gate (hand count
AC1 $= 0.876$, manipulation AC1 $= 0.899$), though its realised median separation was 2.4 hours
rather than the specified seven days (Limitations, \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Estimators.}
Gold-corrected prevalences use PPI++ \citep{angelopoulos2023ppiplusplus} with the control-variate
coefficient $\lambda$ estimated on the gold sample and clipped to $[0,1]$. Agreement uses Gwet's
AC1 \citep{gwet2008ac1} alongside Cohen's $\kappa$. Where cluster identifiers exist (the raw
corpus draws \texttt{S10k-U} and \texttt{S10k-S}), intervals use a cluster bootstrap
($B = 10{,}000$, seed 777) and design effect $(\text{cluster CI width} / \text{iid CI width})^2$;
where no cluster identifier ships (the evaluation arms), registered intervals are iid lower bounds
on width, supplemented by post-hoc cluster-sensitivity analyses (\S\ref{sec:cluster}).

Table~\ref{tab:errdir} presents the audit's primary finding, starting with the vendor's own stored

On the audited $\geq$1-hand indicator, the vendor's own stored labels over-call \textbf{9 of 24}
at-risk frames (37.5\%, 95\% Wilson CI $[21.2, 57.3]$) and under-call \textbf{0 of 129} (0.0\%,
$[0.0, 2.9]$; Fisher's exact $p = 1.3\times 10^{-8}$). On active manipulation, the vendor's labels
over-call \textbf{10 of 33} at-risk frames (30.3\%, $[17.4, 47.3]$) and under-call \textbf{4 of
120} (3.3\%, $[1.3, 8.3]$; $p = 3.4\times 10^{-5}$). Our independent open-weights judge
(\texttt{Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-FP8}) exhibits the same directional asymmetry---slightly weaker on
$\geq$1 hand at \textbf{6 of 24} (25.0\%, $[12.0, 44.9]$) vs.\ \textbf{0 of 129} ($p =
8.3\times 10^{-6}$), and \textbf{10 of 33} (30.3\%) vs.\ \textbf{2 of 120} (1.7\%, $[0.5, 5.9]$;
$p = 3.1\times 10^{-6}$) on manipulation. A further 10 \texttt{Qwen3-VL} disagreements (and 6
vendor over-calls vs.\ 3 under-calls) are one-against-two hand confusions that bear on the
two-hands statistic rather than on $\geq$1 hand.

The directional skew is not uniform across every corpus cell (full per-corpus confusion matrices
appear in Appendix~\ref{app:confusion}). On Egocentric-10K ($n=63$), the vendor's judge over-calls
6 of 9 non-manipulation frames and under-calls 1 of 54, whereas on the EPIC-KITCHENS-100
manipulation cell ($n=60$) the vendor reverses direction (1 over-call vs.\ 2 under-calls).
Because all \vernierNPrimary{} labels come from a single rater and one corpus cell reverses
direction, we report this as strong empirical asymmetry in the gold sample rather than claiming
that VLM-derived prevalences universally form an upper bound; establishing an upper-bound
generalisation requires a second independent rater (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{What kinds of frames trigger disagreements.}
Table~\ref{tab:tags} breaks down all binary-indicator disagreements by the rubric's closed

active manipulation might manufacture judge false positives. That does not occur: \textbf{zero}
manipulation over-calls in either judge carry the \texttt{idle-grip} tag. Instead, over-calls
concentrate on frames showing another person's hands (\texttt{other-person}: 2 of 9 vendor
$\geq$1-hand over-calls and 3 of 10 vendor manipulation over-calls), motion blur (\texttt{blur}:
3 of 10 \texttt{Qwen3-VL} manipulation over-calls), frame-edge cropping, self-contact, or
untagged scenes where the model hallucinates a hand or active task.

Two conclusions follow cleanly. First, Egocentric-10K's lead over Ego4D is large enough (+29.09
and +41.59\,pp published; +36.13 and +37.85\,pp corrected) that judge error does not threaten it.
Second, the comparative claim against EPIC-KITCHENS-100 is \textbf{unresolved}: on active
manipulation, correcting both sides over the vendor's 9{,}800-frame arms moves the lead from
$+6.62$\,pp to $-1.32$\,pp with a 95\% iid interval of $[-10.78, +8.14]$ (open judge: $-1.02$\,pp,
$[-11.68, +9.65]$). Although the point estimate flips sign, the interval comfortably covers both
zero and the published $+6.62$\,pp. An underpowered interval containing the null neither confirms
nor refutes the published margin. On $\geq$1 hand, the vendor-arm margin moves from $+6.05$\,pp to
$+4.71$\,pp ($[-2.31, +11.74]$), likewise unresolved.

Using the exact PPI++ variance decomposition (which holds the unlabelled pool's variance as a
non-shrinking floor), excluding the published $+6.62$\,pp active-manipulation margin at the
current point estimate requires about \textbf{89} gold frames per arm under iid over the vendor's
9{,}800-frame arms---roughly \textbf{55} more labelled frames than the 63 and 60 currently
collected (compared with 226 per arm when using only the 200-frame open-judge pool). As the next
section shows, accounting for clustering raises that requirement further. This margin estimand was
not pre-registered and is reported as exploratory.

\section{Worker and factory clustering}
\label{sec:cluster}

The clustering measurements in this section depend on no human labels. Frames recorded by the same
worker share task, tools, gloves, lighting, and camera geometry; workers inside the same factory
further share workflows and environments. Because the evaluation parquets omit worker and factory
identifiers, we indexed all 19{,}495 raw Egocentric-10K tar shards via HTTP range requests
($\approx 150$\,MB read over a 16\,TB corpus) and drew two independent 10{,}000-frame samples:
\texttt{S10k-U} (uniform over frames; 9{,}989 valid frames across 1{,}966 workers and 85
factories) and \texttt{S10k-S} (stratified by factory worker-hours, $\leq 1$ frame per clip;
9{,}983 valid frames across 1{,}999 workers and 85 factories). Every frame was judged with
\texttt{Qwen3-VL} under \texttt{P0a}, and clustered intervals were computed via cluster bootstrap
($B = 10{,}000$, seed 777).

($\text{deff}_w$) is pre-registered (H2); exact unequal-cluster one-way ANOVA

Table~\ref{tab:deff} reports the iid intervals, worker-clustered intervals, worker design effects,
exact unequal-cluster ANOVA intra-cluster correlations $\hat{\rho}_w$, and post-hoc factory-level
design effects. All six worker design effects exceed 1 (1.25 to 1.66), so worker-aware intervals
are \textbf{12 to 29\% wider} than iid intervals (equivalently, iid intervals understate width by
11 to 22\%). None reaches the pre-registered H2 threshold of 2, so H2 fails as stated while
confirming real within-worker dependence. Two post-hoc findings sharpen the picture
(Appendix~\ref{app:clusters}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Unequal worker-hour skew drives roughly half of the worker design effect.} Worker
      cluster sizes range from 1 to 112 frames ($\bar{m} \approx 5.0$, $\text{CV} \approx
      0.91\text{--}0.97$), so Kish's unequal-cluster factor is $\tilde{m} = \sum m_i^2 / N = 9.27$
      on \texttt{S10k-U} and $9.69$ on \texttt{S10k-S}. Exact one-way random-effects ANOVA yields
      $\hat{\rho}_w = 0.039$ to $0.106$---substantially lower than an equal-cluster back-out would
      imply.
\item \emph{Factory-level clustering is much larger.} Clustering over the 85 factories yields
      design effects of \textbf{2.18 to 5.65} across all six cells, widening intervals by 48 to
      138\% over iid (e.g., on \texttt{S10k-U} active manipulation, iid $[89.21, 90.42]$ widens to
      $[88.73, 90.77]$ at $\text{deff}_f = 2.85$, and 2 hands reaches $\text{deff}_f = 5.65$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Post-hoc cluster sensitivity for the evaluation-arm PPI++ intervals.}
A natural criticism of Table~\ref{tab:vendorprev} and Table~\ref{tab:margins} is that our own
evaluation-arm PPI++ intervals are iid while the paper criticises iid intervals on clustered
frames. Direct Cluster-Robust PPI++ \citep{broska2026crppi} requires cluster IDs on both labelled
and unlabelled units, which Build AI's evaluation parquets do not ship (all 30{,}000 evaluation
frames have null worker, factory, and clip IDs). To quantify how much clustering could widen our
own intervals, Table~\ref{tab:csens} reports two transparent post-hoc sensitivity models beside
the registered iid interval for the primary vendor-arm active-manipulation margin:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model 1 (empirical worker-share scaling + EPIC $K=37$ cluster floor).} On
      Egocentric-10K, multinomial sampling of $n_{\text{gold}}=63$ frames from \texttt{S10k-U}'s
      empirical worker-share distribution ($\sum p_w^2 = 9.28\times 10^{-4}$) barely clusters
      ($\text{deff}_{\text{gold}} = 1.004$), whereas the $N=9{,}800$ unlabelled arm scales by
      $\text{deff}_{\text{unl}} = 1.64$. On EPIC-KITCHENS-100, however, only \textbf{37
      participants} exist (a 58.2$\times$ disparity against Egocentric-10K's 2{,}153 workers,
      pre-registered as H8). Even under equal participant weights, $N=9{,}800$ frames yield
      $\tilde{m} = 265.8$ frames per participant, so at $\rho_{\text{epic}} \in \{0.02,
      \hat{\rho}_{\text{ego}}=0.070, 0.10\}$ the unlabelled variance floor rises from 0.67\,pp
      (iid) to \textbf{1.51--3.04\,pp}, widening the margin CI to $[-11.28, +8.64]$ at matched
      $\rho$ and raising the required gold count to \textbf{93--109} frames per arm. On $\geq$1
      hand, this $K=37$ cluster floor exceeds the gap at all three $\rho$ values, rendering
      separation unachievable by labelling alone.
\item \textbf{Model 2 (conservative direct $\sqrt{\text{deff}_{\max}}$ inflation).} Scaling the
      entire iid standard error on both arms by $\sqrt{1.291} = 1.136$ widens the manipulation
      margin CI to $[-12.07, +9.43]$ and raises the required gold count from 89 to \textbf{115}
      frames per arm.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Prompt divergence and sensitivity.}
Build AI's dataset card (\texttt{P0a}) and shipped prompt file (\texttt{P0b}) differ in four
places, including ``ignore objects held by other people'' versus ``ignore actions performed by
other people.'' At $N = 10{,}000$, the two wordings differ by only 0.32\,pp on Egocentric-10K
active manipulation (0.40\,pp on 100K), well under H1b's 1\,pp threshold. Across eight prompt
variants on 2{,}000 frames (H3), manipulation rates spread 1.25\,pp and hand-count rates 0.25\,pp
against a pre-registered 5\,pp bar; explicitly instructing that gloved hands count (\texttt{P3})
moves the $\geq$1-hand figure by 0.05\,pp. Both judges are thus largely insensitive to surface
prompt wording on these tasks.

\paragraph{Judge--human agreement, domain error rates, distillation, and calibration.}
Across all \vernierNPrimary{} primary labels, \texttt{Qwen3-VL} achieves Gwet's AC1 $= 0.860$
(95\% iid CI $[0.791, 0.921]$, $\kappa = 0.795$) on ternary hand count and $0.887$
($[0.815, 0.945]$, $\kappa = 0.746$) on active manipulation---reversing H4's prediction that hand
count would exceed manipulation. On H5 (pre-registered domain error-rate difference on
manipulation), \texttt{Qwen3-VL} error is 4.76\% on Egocentric-10K ($n=63$) vs.\ 8.33\% on
EPIC-KITCHENS-100 ($n=60$), a $+3.57$\,pp gap in the predicted direction that misses the 5\,pp bar
and is underpowered. A rung-1 linear probe trained on frozen \texttt{facebook/dinov2-small}
features against 29{,}400 stored vendor labels reaches 0.6933 fidelity (against a 0.90 diagnostic
target), and its abstention cascade cannot hold a 0.80 human-agreement floor at any positive
coverage at 95\% confidence on a 46-frame calibration split (H6 fails; the probe is published as a
negative result). Finally, expected calibration error from \texttt{Qwen3-VL} answer-token
logprobs is 0.1043 on hand count and 0.0782 on manipulation, but 99\% of frames fall in the single
$[0.9, 1.0]$ bin under greedy decoding, making calibration degenerate by construction (H7).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Every limitation of this audit is collected here once so the reader can weigh all findings
together:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single human rater and lack of inter-rater reliability.} All \vernierNPrimary{} gold
      labels come from one person (the author), who also wrote the rubric after reading Build AI's
      prompt (introducing unremovable anchoring at $n=1$). There is no inter-rater AC1 or
      $\kappa$. The 34-pair blind retest ran at a median separation of 2.4 hours rather than the
      pre-registered seven days (0 of 34 pairs met the rule), measuring within-session consistency
      rather than recall-free reliability. Sixty labels were collected after initial results were
      public at 3\,s/frame median (versus 10\,s/frame on the first 93), though batch sensitivity
      shows the error asymmetry persists without the fast labels (Table~\ref{tab:sens}).
\item \textbf{No upper-bound generalisation before a second rater.} Because the gold set is
      single-rater and the vendor's stored labels reverse direction on the EPIC-KITCHENS-100
      manipulation cell (1 over-call vs.\ 2 under-calls), we do not claim that VLM-derived corpus
      prevalences universally serve as upper bounds.
\item \textbf{Missing evaluation cluster identifiers.} Evaluation parquets ship bare UUID4 frame
      IDs with no worker, factory, or participant linkage. All evaluation-arm PPI++ intervals are
      therefore iid lower bounds on width; our cluster-sensitivity intervals
      (Table~\ref{tab:csens}) rely on raw-corpus worker distributions and assumed EPIC-KITCHENS-100
      participant ICCs rather than direct Cluster-Robust PPI++ \citep{broska2026crppi}, and H2's
      design effects are measured on our raw-corpus draws rather than on Build AI's evaluation
      sample.
\item \textbf{Judge lineage and pretraining exposure.} \texttt{Qwen3-VL} and
      \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} may share pretraining data and architectural inductive biases, so
      their agreement is an upper bound on judge reliability. Moreover, Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS-100
      are public datasets plausibly seen during VLM pretraining, whereas gated Egocentric-10K is
      not---a confound on cross-domain error comparisons that we did not probe.
\item \textbf{Exploratory margin estimand and uncovered SLA axes.} The comparative margin
      estimand (\S\ref{sec:prevalence}) was not pre-registered. Build AI's advertised 3D-pose
      MPJPE guarantee cannot be audited because public releases ship no pose annotations, and a
      planned matched three-corpus downstream transfer probe was blocked by institutional email
      requirements for EPIC-KITCHENS-100 and absent downstream labels in the evaluation release.
      Whether hand visibility predicts downstream training value remains untested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendations, ethics, and reproducibility}
\label{sec:ethics}

\paragraph{Four low-cost upgrades for dataset producers.}
Turning a single-pass VLM marketing statistic into an auditable quality guarantee requires four
inexpensive steps: (1) publish a cluster-aware confidence interval alongside every headline rate;
(2) validate against a small, multi-rater human gold set and report the \emph{direction} of judge
error (over-calling vs.\ under-calling) rather than only aggregate accuracy; (3) ship stable
frame-to-worker and frame-to-site identifiers in evaluation releases so external auditors can
verify sampling frames and run Cluster-Robust PPI++ \citep{broska2026crppi}; and (4) version the
exact prompt and model snapshot used to produce the published table. Re-running an independent
open-weights judge costs roughly \$9 per 10{,}000 frames on commodity GPU capacity and requires no
proprietary API.

\paragraph{Ethics, frame withdrawal, and vendor notice.}
This PDF embeds no raw video frames. Under our provenance policy (\texttt{docs/ETHICS.md}~\S4),
frames are redistributed only in one withdrawable thumbnail atlas inside the interactive Space
(24 manually likeness-reviewed Egocentric-10K frames showing no person other than the camera
wearer, licensed Apache-2.0 by Build AI; Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS-100 frames are never
redistributed, and 30 later-labelled Egocentric-10K frames remain excluded pending manual likeness
review). Because an archival PDF cannot honour per-frame withdrawal requests, we report the
structured disagreement-tag breakdown in Table~\ref{tab:tags} and link the Space for interactive
inspection. Worker and factory identifiers are used exclusively as statistical clustering units;
no per-worker result is reported, and no claim is made regarding the undisclosed consent
instruments behind the corpus. Publishing without prior vendor contact was a pre-registered
independence choice (no security or privacy exposure was found); a follow-up design
(\texttt{docs/DESIGN-V2.md}) specifies independent second-rater validation, confirmatory margin
registration, and vendor notification with a right of reply.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Single-pass VLM quality metrics published without intervals or human calibration conceal three
measurement hazards that show up directly on Egocentric-10K. First, against our \vernierNPrimary{}-frame
single-rater gold set, the vendor's own stored \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} labels over-call $\geq$1
hand on 9 of 24 at-risk frames while under-calling 0 of 129, and over-call active manipulation on
10 of 33 against 4 of 120---an asymmetry corroborated by an open-weights judge across both tasks.
Second, video frames are clustered by camera wearer and factory: worker clustering widens
intervals by 12 to 29\% (factory clustering by 48 to 138\%), yet omitting cluster identifiers from
evaluation releases prevents anyone outside the vendor from computing cluster-robust intervals on
the published sample. Third, correcting both sides of the active-manipulation lead over
EPIC-KITCHENS-100 shifts it from $+6.62$ to $-1.32$\,pp ($[-10.78, +8.14]$ iid, wider under
clustering), leaving the comparative claim unresolved. Closing that gap does not require a newer
model judge: it requires an independent second rater on the 153 gold frames, roughly 55 to 110
additional gold labels per arm, and worker identifiers shipped alongside evaluation frames.
